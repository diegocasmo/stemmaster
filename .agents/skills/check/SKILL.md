---
name: check
description: Check that a song's recipe records every chain move for every source, and compute the measured report reviewers judge it by. Use before any mix or master review.
---

- Run `uv run .agents/skills/check/check.py [--master | --review-prompt] songs/<id>/recipe.json`. It writes `renders/check/report.md` and `report.json`, prints every `review:` question and `fail:`, and exits 1 on any failure. `--no-report` runs only the structural checks.
- Run long checks in the background and wait for that job's own completion notice and exit status.
- Progress and elapsed seconds go to stderr. Never wait with a `pgrep -f` loop: it can match its own shell.
- It lints the `### <chain>.<move>` headings of `docs/mixing-chains/` and reads each move's **Tool** list.
- `sources` names each instrument: `{"kick": {"chain": "kick", "stems": [...], "timing": "03_KickSample.wav"}}`.
  - Every audio file in `stems/` is in one source or in `excluded`, as `"vocal"` or `"di"` (a DI track, which is never used). Every recipe stem is in a source.
  - The chain must match the file name (kick, snare, tom, overhead/hat/cymbal/room, bass, guitar, synth/pad/keys/sfx), or the source gives `chain_reason`.
  - Each tom is its own source. Any other chain has one source, unless each of its sources gives `split_reason`.
  - A source with several stems routes them to one bus that nothing else feeds: its node, where the report measures it.
  - `timing` is the cleanest stem to find drum hits on, a sample stem if there is one. Hits are found on it unprocessed, at its offset.
  - Stem roles come from file names (top, bottom, sample, in, out, sub, hat, spot, room, di, amp, lead; other guitars are rhythm). `roles` changes one only with `role_reason`, printed for review. A synths source gives `role`: bass, lead or pad. A bass synth also gets every bass move on that source, and a lead synth gets `guitars.lead`, read against the guitars' rhythms.
- `moves` records every move of every source's chain, plus `drum-bus` (when there are drums), `mix-bus`, and with `--master` `master`: `{"move": "kick.gate", "source": "kick", "decision": "kept", "steps": ["gate-kick"], "fields": [], "note": "..."}`.
  - `decision` is kept, changed or dropped. A dropped move claims nothing. `note` gives the measured reason.
  - kept and changed claim at least one step whose plugin (or Airwindows `effect`) is in the move's Tool list, or a field its Tool names. `Gain` steps may ride along. Anything else needs `tool_reason`, which the check prints for the reviewers.
  - `fields` are `stems.<file>.invert`, `.offset_samples`, `.pan`, `.gain_db`, `.bus`, `buses.<bus>.bus`, and sends as `stems.<file>.sends.<bus>` or `buses.<bus>.sends.<bus>`.
  - Every step, every set `invert`, `offset_samples` and `pan`, and every send is claimed by at least one record. Routing (`bus`) may be claimed, as by `drum-bus.groups`; each source's node already checks it. Several records may claim one shared step, as each tom claims the tom-bus compressor.
  - A record acts only on its source's stems and buses fed only by its chain's stems. A `Duck` crosses sources only for kick.duck-bass (kick key, bass target), overheads.snare-control (snare, overheads) and guitars.lead-room (lead, rhythms), recorded on the key's source. `drum-bus` acts on buses fed by several drum chains and on drum returns. `mix-bus` and `master` act only on `mix`.
  - Master moves are rejected until `--master`. They claim only master-stage steps, and with `--master` every master-stage step belongs to a master move, not the trial master.
  - A guitars.align record names its tracks of one take as `take`, the reference first, including every stem it moves. Its sum gain is read on their notes; a pair whose best correlation (level-free) is under 0.25 is not one take.
  - `{"move": "balance", "steps": [...], "fields": [...], "note": "..."}` claims `Gain` steps and stem `gain_db` or `pan` that no chain move covers.
- Every move has a contract in `contracts.py`: the signals, events, bands and passages its Needed if and Worked if read, as leaves. Needed if is read without the move (its off render for kept and changed records, the current mix for dropped ones). Worked if compares with and without it.
  - Passages: fast and sparse from the hit rate (a drum's own hits, else kick and snare), in 4 s windows; with no clear contrast the busy part of the song is one passage. Moves comparing section groups need `sections` with `group`; `mix-bus.rides` needs `loudness_lu`. A section is read in 3 s windows from its start where the part plays; one it rests through is left out, and one it only rings into or picks up out of (under 1.5 s) is a review question.
  - Notes (bass, guitars, synths) are found on each played stem by spectral flux, and each is read only up to the next note. Palm mutes can't be told from open chords on an amp track, so those leaves are review questions.
  - Each leaf is true, false, unresolved (no supported evidence), review (the doc gives no number: a question the reviewers must answer) or skipped (no profile).
  - Default outcome: no fault → dropped; fault and it worked → kept; fault and it didn't → dropped.
- The check fails a record when its outcome is unresolved, when its decision contradicts the default outcome, when a fault was found and the move was dropped without a trial, and when a claimed step or trial does nothing (under -40 dB residual after aligning and matching it to its own input).
  - `override: {"reason": "...", "leaves": [ids]}` accepts a contradiction only when it names every leaf that decided it. It never answers unresolved evidence or an ineffective step. Overrides go to the reviewers.
  - Always-tried moves dropped are judged by the check's own starting trial (`DEFAULT_TRIALS`). Another dropped move whose Needed if finds a fault needs `trial`: `{"steps": [{..., "before" or "after": <step id>}], "buses": {...}, "sends": {<own stem or bus>: [...]}, "routing": {<own stem>: <bus>}}`. A record trial that fails needs an override naming its failing leaves.
  - Every variant's trial master is solved to the profile's `master_integrated_lufs`, so limiter dip and crest are compared at release loudness. With no profile those leaves are unresolved and profile-range leaves skipped.
- The report also renders the recipe once without each kept or changed record's claims and lists every number that moved: per source node and per other bus a step acts on, the mix (bands, width, valley, overlaps) and the trial master (dip, loudness, true peak, profile range).
- Results are cached in `renders/check/` by everything they depend on: recipe, hits, code, stems, plugins and profile.
- Mix review: `--review-prompt` writes `renders/check/review-prompt.md` and prints a `reviews` entry. Send the prompt, unchanged, as the first message of one fresh Claude and one fresh Codex session through Herdr; each ends with the json verdict block it asks for, answering every question. Add their session ids to the entry. `--master` then requires that entry: the same mix fingerprint, a pass from both transcripts on this machine, and every question answered. Any mix change after review needs a new review. Each review session holds only the prompt from the user. A hand-written transcript, or steering a reviewer through its own instruction files or Codex developer instructions, is not detected; the binding stops a session that was told its verdict in a message.
